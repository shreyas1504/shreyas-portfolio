01
Where This Started

Not a textbook problem.
I watched it happen.

During my SDE internship building the SMARTeIS multi-tenant invoicing platform at Skill Quotient Group, I was in the same Slack channels as the PM team. Every Monday morning, the PM wrote the same project context three times: once in a Jira comment, once in a stakeholder email, and once in a Slack update to the Eng lead.

Nothing was wrong. The PM was competent. The tools worked fine. But the tools didn't talk to each other, didn't remember context, and couldn't draft anything. Every week started from zero.

"I'm basically a human ETL pipeline. I ingest status from five places and output the same summary six times a week."

- PM, Skill Quotient Group

That sentence became the seed of this case study. Eight user interviews later, I had a clear structural picture of where PM time goes and how to solve it without sacrificing human trust.

02
Discovery & Research

What I actually
learned from PMs.

I ran 8 semi-structured interviews over two weeks with 5 PMs through NYU's MOT program, 2 from my internship context, and 1 cold outreach at a B2B SaaS company. My goal was to understand where time actually goes, not validate a pre-existing hypothesis.

Research Methodology

// Interview Protocol
Format
30 min Zoom, semi-structured. Started with "walk me through last Tuesday" - open-ended, no leading
Sample
n=8. Mix: 3 mid-level PMs, 2 PM leads, 1 APM, 1 TPM, 1 startup founder-PM. All in B2B SaaS
Analysis
Affinity mapping in FigJam. Clustered pain points, looked for Jobs-to-be-Done language
Bias check
Asked "what's going right with your tools?" to avoid confirmation bias before hitting pain points

Key Findings - What I Didn't Expect

Pain #1 - Most Common

Status communication is the real killer

7 of 8 PMs mentioned this unprompted. Writing the same project status for different audiences (engineering, leadership, sales, customers) took 3–5 hours/week.

Pain #2 - Most Nuanced

Context loss is an invisible tax

"Why did we decide X six months ago?" was a question PMs either couldn't answer or spent 20 minutes tracing through Slack and old docs to find.

Surprise Finding

PMs don't want AI to replace them - they want a first draft

When I asked "would you want AI to write your status updates automatically?", 6/8 said no - they feared losing their voice. But "would you want AI to draft it and you edit?" got unanimous yes.

Opportunity Unlocked

Onboarding is 3–6 weeks of lost productivity

New PMs spend their first month trying to understand why decisions were made. An AI that could answer "what's the context behind epic #247?" is immediately valuable.

Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

// Core Jobs to be Done
Job 1
When I need to update stakeholders, help me communicate status without starting from scratch, so I don't lose 30 minutes writing the same thing I wrote last week.
Job 2
When a stakeholder asks why we made a decision, help me surface the original rationale fast, so I don't spend an hour archaeology-ing through Slack.
Job 3
When a new PM joins my team, help them understand our product context in days, not weeks, without interrupting the rest of the team constantly.
Job 4
When engineering asks about roadmap priorities, help me answer confidently and consistently with cited specs.
03
Problem Definition

The tools aren't broken.
They're blind.

Jira, Notion, Confluence, and Slack are all good at tracking, documenting, and communicating. But none of them understand product context. They're static silos.

Before - The Reality
  • 4–5 hrs/week writing status updates from scratch for different audiences
  • 20+ minutes tracing a decision through Slack threads and old docs
  • 3–6 weeks for a new PM to get full product context from the team
  • PM becomes the synchronous bottleneck for all stakeholder questions
  • Decision rationale decays - nobody remembers "why" after 3 months
After - The Vision
  • Draft generated in 90 seconds, PM reviews and edits for 5 minutes
  • Ask in plain English: "Why did we defer push notifications?" → instant answer with citations
  • New PMs query the AI on context, onboard 3x faster
  • Stakeholders get a self-serve portal for roadmap questions - no PM required
  • Every decision auto-logged with context, searchable forever

05
System Architecture

How I'd actually
build this.

The co-pilot is a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system that sits on top of your existing product stack, builds a permission-gated semantic index, and answers queries against that live index with citations.

Fine-tuning - Why I Ruled It Out

Fine-tuning bakes a snapshot of your product context into model weights. When the roadmap changes next sprint, your model is out of date, and you can't point to the source of an answer.

RAG - Why It Fits

RAG retrieves live documents at query time. Every answer cites a specific source the PM can verify. Permission-gating happens at vector retrieval time, not just at the UI layer.

2 - Retrieve

Human edits, approves, sends. Always.
06
UX & Interactive Frame

Design for
humans reviewing AI,
not AI replacing humans.

The co-pilot always drafts, the PM always approves. This is the product's core trust mechanism in an enterprise environment.

Principle 1

AI drafts, humans send

No output goes to any stakeholder without explicit PM approval. The "Send" action is always human-triggered.

Principle 2

Every answer has a source

All AI responses surface inline citations with clickable links to the source Jira ticket, Slack thread, or PRD.

Principle 3

Uncertainty is visible

The system shows a confidence score with every output based on corroborating retrieved sources.

09
What I Learned

What building this
actually taught me.

01
Trust is a product feature, not a disclaimer
Trust in B2B AI is earned through mechanisms: visible citations, editable outputs, human gates before any external send, and audit trails accessible to leadership.
02
My first scope was 4x too big
My original doc had 12 features in the MVP. After applying MoSCoW honestly, I cut it to 2: Status Update Drafter + Decision History Q&A.
03
Architecture decisions are PM decisions
Choosing RAG over fine-tuning determines your accuracy maintenance costs, data privacy posture, explainability, and staleness when the roadmap changes.
04
The "PM as editor" model unlocked adoption thinking
Framing the co-pilot as "AI that drafts, PM that edits" turned PMs who feared being replaced into PMs excited about getting their Monday mornings back.
